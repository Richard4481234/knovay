GeoProof · Interactive Geometry

Proofs you can grab and move

GeoProof turns the classic results of geometry into living diagrams. Every explorer shows a construction — a triangle, a circle, an inscribed angle, a set of tangents — where the key points are draggable. Grab a vertex and move it, and the whole figure updates instantly while the theorem keeps holding true: the angles of a triangle still sum to 180°, an inscribed angle stays exactly half its central angle, the nine-point circle keeps passing through all nine points. Because every measurement is recomputed live to machine precision, you're not staring at a fixed textbook drawing — you're stress-testing the theorem for yourself.

The library spans 73 explorers across triangles, circles, angles, quadrilaterals, polygons and transformations — from the Pythagorean theorem to Ptolemy, Ceva, the Euler line and 3-D solids. You can search and filter by shape or difficulty, save favourites, follow guided paths that sequence topics into a lesson, and test yourself in a 625-question quiz with retry-missed and adaptive rounds.

For students

Build real intuition by experimenting instead of memorising. Drag the figure until a proof finally "clicks," then check your understanding with the quiz — all free in the browser, with nothing to install.

For teachers

Drop a live, projector-ready demonstration into any lesson, or embed an explorer straight into your notes or LMS. One tool covers a full curriculum of theorems, with a quiz for practice and quick assessment.

Physica · Interactive Physics Lab

A physics lab that runs in your browser

Physica is a collection of 67 hands-on experiments organised into six fields — mechanical, thermal, electromagnetism, optical, modern and fluid. Open one and it behaves like a real bench: press play, then tune the numbers — mass, launch angle, temperature, charge, wavelength — and watch the outcome unfold in real time. Crucially, each simulation is driven by the actual governing equations, so what you see is genuine physics rather than a canned animation: projectiles trace true parabolas, gas molecules reproduce the Maxwell–Boltzmann speed curve, and light through two slits builds up the real interference pattern.

It reaches from everyday mechanics — projectile motion, collisions, pendulums, orbits and Kepler's laws — to the invisible and the exotic: Coulomb field lines, electromagnetic waves, lenses and diffraction, the photoelectric effect, the Bohr atom, special relativity and even a black hole. You can browse by field, search all 67, and star the experiments you want to return to.

For students

See the invisible — fields, waves and forces — made visible, and safely run experiments that would be impossible or expensive in a real lab. Change one variable at a time to feel how the equations actually behave.

For teachers

Demonstrate any topic live in a lecture, or set the simulators as at-home exploration and virtual lab work. The six fields span most of a physics syllabus, ready to use with no setup or equipment.

Calculo · Interactive Calculus

Calculus you can see move

Calculo turns the ideas of calculus — the mathematics of change — into things you can drag. Every lab is a small experiment: slide a point along a curve and watch the tangent's slope trace out the whole derivative; add rectangles under a graph and see the Riemann sum squeeze toward the exact integral; walk two probes toward a point and decide whether the limit exists. Nothing is a canned animation — each explorer recomputes the real quantities live, so the slope reads 4.00 exactly where it should and the area lands on its true value. You don't memorise the rule; you watch it happen.

The set spans 23 explorers covering an entire first-year course: limits and the ε–δ definition, derivatives, the chain rule, optimization, related rates and Newton's method; then integrals, area between curves, volumes by disks and shells, arc length and the Fundamental Theorem; and on to Taylor series, infinite series, parametric & polar curves and slope fields. Each runs in a single, distraction-free page with nothing to install.

For students

Get past the symbols by playing with them. Drag first and read the numbers second — when the tangent goes flat exactly where the curve peaks, the meaning of f′(x)=0 lands on its own. It's the whole syllabus, free in the browser.

For teachers

Project a live derivative, integral or limit and let students watch it respond in real time, or hand them a lab to explore before class. One tool covers differential and integral calculus, ready to use with no setup.
